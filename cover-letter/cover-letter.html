      When to send one, and how Notes and checklist

      When to use it

      • Send one: startups and companies under ~200 people, remote-first companies, direct emails to a founder or engineering manager, Hacker News "Who is hiring" replies, and any posting that asks for one.
      • Skip it: optional fields on big-company portals, LinkedIn Easy Apply, and vetting platforms (Lemon.io, Toptal, G2i). They don't read them. Exception: if the field says a person reads it ("We are real humans looking at applications"), fill it with the short answer.
      • Screening questions instead of a letter? Answer the questions and skip the letter.
      • Brazil: most companies don't ask for one. Use the Portuguese version in Gupy's "Apresente-se!" field (a person reads it; the AI ranking doesn't), in optional fields on career pages, and as the email body when a posting says "envie seu currículo". For email, turn on the subject line and attach the CV as a PDF. Gupy's field fits 1,500 characters.
      • Format: paste it as plain text into the form or the email body. No bold, bullets or em dashes. Use a PDF only when there's nothing but an upload slot.

      Never send it with blanks left in, and never without the company-specific sentence. Anyone can generate a polished generic letter now, so it counts for almost nothing. What a reader notices is the company-specific sentence and the details your CV can't show.

      Picking the story

      Keep one story per letter. The ERP rewrite is the default: it covers front-end and full-stack React and business software, and it carries the point about AI-written code checked by tests. Switch to the video-surveillance product for Python, AI or product roles, and to the school-transport platform for Node.js / PostgreSQL full-stack or startup roles.

      Salary goes in only if the posting asks: one line, as a range, saying CLT, PJ, or "CLT ou PJ" when you have no preference and the listing doesn't say.

      "Why do you want to work here?"

      Always write this one fresh: one specific thing about the company, then the closest thing you've actually done.

      Before sending

      1. Every blank filled, and the "why this company" sentence fails the competitor-swap test.
      2. No em dashes, bold, bullets, or words like "passionate", "thrive", "align", "leverage", "robust", "delve". The checks under the letter catch these.
      3. No adjectives about yourself. Every claim is something you can explain on a call, screen by screen.
      4. Read it out loud once and change anything you wouldn't say.
      5. Free-text fields a person reads: about 100 words. A reader skims anything longer.
